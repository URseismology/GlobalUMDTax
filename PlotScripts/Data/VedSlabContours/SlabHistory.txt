# REPORT: THE THREE-PHASE MESOZOIC SUBDUCTION SYSTEM (252 – 66 Ma)
# TOPIC: Pangea Breakup, Panthalassa Evolution, and the Transition to Modern Plate Tectonics

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
The Mesozoic Era (252 – 66 Ma) represents the structural transition from a geodynamic regime dominated by a single insulated supercontinent (Pangea) surrounded by a global superocean (Panthalassa) to the fragmented, high-mobility plate framework characteristic of the modern Earth. Rather than using traditional biostratigraphic epoch boundaries, this report delineates Mesozoic tectonics into a three-phase system governed by the evolution of global subduction zones, specifically using 175 Ma and 120 Ma as the key thresholds of geodynamic reorganization.

---

## PHASE 1: THE INSULATED SUPERCONTINENT & STABLE SUBDUCTION GIRDLE (252 Ma – 175 Ma)

### 1. Geodynamic Configuration
During the Triassic and Early Jurassic, the core configuration of Earth consisted of the C-shaped supercontinent Pangea locked internally by a massive, near-continuous peripheral ring of downwelling mantle material known as the "subduction girdle." This girdle was driven by the continuous subduction of old, dense oceanic lithosphere from the Panthalassa superocean beneath the outer margins of the supercontinent.

### 2. Subduction Mechanisms & Mantle Behavior
* Subduction Girdle Isolation: The continuous ring of circum-Pangean subduction zones effectively anchored the supercontinent in place, severely limiting lateral continental drift across the globe.
* Thermal Isolation Engine: Pangea acted as a massive, continental thermal blanket. Because heat escaping from the core-mantle boundary was trapped beneath this giant unmoving lithospheric shield, the sub-continental mantle underwent progressive superheating.
* Asthenospheric Dynamics: The wedge-shaped geometry of the ancient Tethyan oceanic realm, situated inside the "C" of Pangea, acted as the primary escape hatch for the trapped, pressurized sub-Pangea asthenosphere.

### 3. The Reorganization Threshold (~175 Ma)
The continuous buildup of thermal buoyancy and mantle overpressure beneath the Pangean lithospheric armor reached a critical threshold in the Early-Middle Jurassic. The stress manifested as severe crustal extension and major Large Igneous Province (LIP) volcanism (e.g., the Central Atlantic Magmatic Province, or CAMP), culminating in the opening of the Central Atlantic ocean basin. This structural breach tore the first major gap in the stable subduction girdle.

---

## PHASE 2: PERIPHERAL FAILURE, MASSIVE DRIFT, & INTERNAL RIFTING (175 Ma – 120 Ma)

### 1. Geodynamic Configuration
This phase captures the mid-Mesozoic fragmentation window. The rigid, top-down constraints of the Paleo-Pacific subduction girdle failed systematically as Pangea broke into Laurasia (north) and Gondwana (south), which then further shattered into smaller individual blocks.

### 2. Subduction Mechanisms & Mantle Behavior
* Slab-Pull Traction: Subduction dynamics during this phase were characterized by powerful slab-pull forces localized along the northern Tethyan trench systems. This intense downward pull rapidly dragged fragmented southern blocks—including India, Africa, and Australia—northward toward Eurasia, systematically shrinking the Tethys Ocean.
* Cordilleran Accretionary Systems: While subduction along the southern and eastern margins broke apart, subduction along the western margin of the Americas (the Cordilleran margin) intensified. This driven oceanic subduction led to the widespread accretion of exotic island arcs and microcontinents onto western North and South America.
* Intraoceanic Subduction Reorganization: Seismic tomography reveals that deep within the Panthalassa (Paleo-Pacific) domain, massive intraoceanic subduction zones formed (preserved today as slab remnants beneath the Nazca and Pacific plates), splitting the lower mantle large low-shear-velocity provinces (LLSVPs).

### 3. The Reorganization Threshold (~120 Ma)
By the Early Cretaceous, the network of old subduction zones reached a major structural tipping point. The ancient plate boundary configuration along the southern margin of the old supercontinent underwent a terminal collapse. This occurred when major mid-ocean ridges (such as the Pacific-Aluk spreading center) were subducted directly into the trenches near the Australia-New Zealand-Antarctica margins, fundamentally changing the balance of torques operating on the global plate network.

---

## PHASE 3: THE CRETACEOUS REVOLUTION & THE MODERN PACIFIC PATTERN (120 Ma – 66 Ma)

### 1. Geodynamic Configuration
The final phase of the Mesozoic marks the complete dissolution of Pangea-style tectonics and the establishment of the modern plate mosaic. This interval is defined globally as the "Cretaceous Revolution."

### 2. Subduction Mechanisms & Mantle Behavior
* Lateral Mantle Mixing: With the old subduction girdle completely dismantled, deep lateral mixing of the upper mantle commenced. The long-term accumulation of sub-continental heat was released globally, driving hyper-accelerated seafloor spreading rates and an unprecedented rise in global sea levels.
* Flat-Slab Tectonic Overdrive: The newborn Pacific Plate underwent extreme velocity changes and expanded rapidly. Along western North America, the subduction angle of the Farallon plate shifted from steep to flat. This flat-slab subduction reached deep beneath the continental interior, transferring horizontal stress that compressed the crust and forced up the ancestral Rocky Mountains (Laramide Orogeny) and the Sierra Nevada.
* Latitudinal Migration: Subduction zones migrated away from high-latitude polar sinks and stabilized along lower-latitude paths, outlining the early geometry of what is now the modern Pacific Ring of Fire.

## COMPARATIVE SUMMARY MATRIX

| Tectonic Parameter | Phase 1 (252 – 175 Ma) | Phase 2 (175 – 120 Ma) | Phase 3 (120 – 66 Ma) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Core Subduction Style | Stable, circum-Pangean subduction girdle. | Fragmenting arcs; heavy Tethyan slab pull. | Global low-latitude migration; flat-slab dynamics. |
| Mantle State | Sub-Pangean mantle insulation & superheating. | Deep intraoceanic slab downwelling begins. | Complete lateral mixing; rapid thermal dissipation. |
| Continental Mobility | Stationarity; minimal lateral drift. | Active separation of Laurasia and Gondwana. | High-velocity dispersal toward modern positions. |
| Oceanic Expression | Panthalassa dominance; early Atlantic rifting. | Tethys closure; birth of early Pacific plate. | Expansion of Pacific Plate; Central/South Atlantic open. |

## BIBLIOGRAPHY & ANNOTATED REFERENCES

1. Le Pichon, X., & Francheteau, J. (2023). Breakup of Pangea and the Cretaceous Revolution. Tectonics, 42(1), e2022TC007489.
   - Detailing the mechanical role of Pangea's lithospheric armor, the operations of the 260–180 Ma subduction girdle, mantle thermal isolation, and the mid-Cretaceous shift toward modern, globally homogeneous plate behavior.

2. Isozaki, Y. (2021). The Mesozoic superocean Panthalassa: A review of its subduction history and sedimentary record. Journal of Asian Earth Sciences, 214, 104780.
   - Comprehensive documentation on how the continuous margins of Panthalassa transitioned from stable late-Paleozoic lines into fragmented Mesozoic convergent zones.

3. Seton, M., Müller, R. D., Zahirovic, S., Williams, S., & Wright, N. M. (2015). Mesozoic and Cenozoic global plate reconstructions with evolving plate boundaries. Earth-Science Reviews, 151, 1-32.
   - Providing quantified global plate kinematic datasets that capture the shifting velocities of the Tethyan and Pacific subduction systems across the 175 Ma and 120 Ma boundaries.

4. Wu, L., Mitchell, R. N., Murphy, J. B., & Pisarevsky, S. A. (2024). Mesozoic intraoceanic subduction shaped the lower mantle: Insights from seismic tomography. Earth and Planetary Science Letters, 626, 118512.
   - Utilizing SS precursor data and mantle tomography to map ancient 250 to 120 Ma intraoceanic slabs that disrupted the lower mantle beneath the modern Pacific and Nazca plates.

5. Torsvik, T. H., Van der Voo, R., Doubrovine, P. V., Burke, K., Steinberger, B., Ashwal, L. D., ... & Webb, S. J. (2014). Deep mantle structure as a reference frame for movements in a Pangea supercontinent. Solid Earth, 5(2), 495-516.
   - Linking surface plate configurations during the Pangean lifecycle directly to downwelling zones and Large Low Shear Velocity Provinces (LLSVPs) at the core-mantle boundary.
